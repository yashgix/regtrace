Why it matters

Part of compliance change work can be checked mechanically

Medicaid health plans turn federal changes into policy edits, owners and audit evidence. Three parts of that work can be checked without judgment: which rule text changed, when the change appears in the eCFR, and whether a quoted citation was in force on a date.

  • Point-in-time text. Every section and paragraph as of any snapshot date, with CFR-style labels.
  • Explicit matching rules. Each status has a written definition, so every row can be rechecked by hand.
  • A citation checker that fails closed. A quote is valid only if it is in that paragraph on that date. In a self-check over every paragraph of 20 characters or more, each paragraph's own text checks as valid, and the same quote with its last word changed is rejected.

regtrace is independent of Readily and uses public federal data only. It makes no claim about Readily's customers, data, accuracy or implementation.

Limits

What this does not show

  • The scores measure agreement with eCFR's own version history on one pinned snapshot. They are not accuracy against verified amendments.
  • The unexplained list is changes no parsed instruction claims. It is not a count of untagged amendments and not a claim that the eCFR is wrong.
  • Applicability dates inside rule text are not parsed. Only federal rules are covered, with no state guidance.
  • The eCFR is an editorial compilation, not the official legal edition.

Technical details

Method, data and results

Four jobs, one module each

  1. labels.py turns eCFR XML into CFR-style labeled paragraphs such as (b)(1)(i). Unusual forms fall back to an unlabeled ~n form rather than a guessed label.
  2. diff.py diffs two labeled snapshots into ordered change records.
  3. fr.py parses Federal Register amendatory instructions inside REGTEXT into section claims.
  4. reconcile.py and cite.py reconcile those claims against eCFR section versions and verify quoted citations.

A read-only FastAPI app serves sections at a date, changes between dates, documents with their rows, the unexplained list and POST /verify.

What the statuses mean

StatusRule
matchedThe eCFR has a substantive version of the section on the rule's effective date, or its publication date when there is no effective date.
shiftedNo version on that date, but one between publication and 30 days after the expected date. The nearest date wins.
before_historyBoth dates are on or before 2017-01-01, so the eCFR cannot witness the change.
unmatchedNone of the above.
unexplainedA substantive eCFR version, after the base date, that no row claims.

Precision is the share of predicted rows that show a substantive eCFR change near the rule's dates. Recall is the share of substantive eCFR changes that some row claims. Both measure agreement with eCFR's own history on this snapshot. They do not show whether a predicted section was actually amended.

Data

upstream/ is a pinned snapshot of US federal works in the public domain: the full eCFR XML of Part 438 for each of 38 dates on which any section changed, eCFR section versions, and the Federal Register documents tagged to 42 CFR 438 since 2016, with full text where available. Every file's source URL, SHA-256 and size are in upstream/MANIFEST.json. Tests and the API never use the network.
